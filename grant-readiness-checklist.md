# Grant Readiness Checklist

Work top to bottom. Most small nonprofits don't lose grants on prose — they
lose them on readiness: a lapsed registration, a missing financial, a deadline
nobody tracked. Fix every box **before** you pick a funder, not the week an
LOI is due.

> This checklist reflects common grantmaking practice. It is not legal advice
> and it does not replace the funder's own guidelines — **the funder's
> eligibility rules and required attachments always override this file.**

## 1. Legal and registrations

- [ ] IRS determination letter (501(c)(3) or equivalent) on file and legible
- [ ] Entity in good standing with the state (registered agent, annual report current)
- [ ] State charitable-solicitation registration current in every state where you fundraise
- [ ] UEI from SAM.gov (required for most US federal and many pass-through grants)
- [ ] W-9 signed and dated within the last year
- [ ] Conflict-of-interest policy adopted by the board

## 2. Financial readiness

- [ ] Board-approved current-year operating budget
- [ ] Most recent IRS Form 990 filed and publicly posted (funders check)
- [ ] Financial statements for the last two fiscal years (reviewed or audited if your size or funders require it)
- [ ] Bookkeeping system that can produce a program-level expense report on request
- [ ] Bank letter or voided check for grant deposits
- [ ] Indirect cost approach decided (a flat % inside the program budget, or a negotiated rate)

## 3. Story and evidence

- [ ] Mission statement in one sentence, board-endorsed
- [ ] Needs statement with **cited** local data (not "studies show") — sources and dates recorded
- [ ] One-paragraph program description per program: who, what, when, where
- [ ] Population served described with numbers you can defend (unduplicated counts, last period)
- [ ] Two to four outcomes stated in measurable terms ("X% of participants will…")
- [ ] Last cycle's results, including the unflattering ones — funders ask

## 4. People and governance

- [ ] Current board roster with affiliations
- [ ] Bios for the ED and the people who will run the funded program
- [ ] Org chart, even a simple one
- [ ] ED and program lead calendars blocked for writing and funder calls in application months

## 5. Capacity and process

- [ ] A named grant owner (one person accountable; a committee owns nothing)
- [ ] A single deadline tracker — every prospect, amount, due date, and required attachment in one place
- [ ] Attachments folder assembled once and reused: 990, budget, board list, determination letter, W-9, bios
- [ ] Template for letters of support (community partners sign, you draft)
- [ ] Reporting calendar: know what you'll owe each funder **before** you take their money

## 6. Red flags — fix before applying

- [ ] More than 25% of revenue from a single funder and falling
- [ ] A 990 with missing schedules or late filing
- [ ] No restricted-fund tracking (comingled grant money is how goodwill dies)
- [ ] Program outcomes you cannot currently measure
- [ ] Applying to a funder whose last grants were 10× your annual budget — sizing mismatch reads as unreadiness
